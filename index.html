01

During craving

A craving is not only in the mind. The body changes too.

1

What happens to the body

The body reacts as it does to danger. Heart rate and sweating rise within seconds.

Open

What happens to the body

Set off by stress, a reminder of past use, or withdrawal. The body reacts as it does to danger.

↑

Heart rate

Beats faster within seconds. One of the clearest findings.

↑

Sweating

Starts within seconds and can be measured on the skin.

↓

Heart rate variability

The gaps between heartbeats become more even.

↑

Breathing rate

Tends to get faster. Studied less than the others.

↓

Skin temperature

Falls slightly as hands and wrists cool.

↔

Restlessness

Common in craving and withdrawal.

The same changes happen with everyday stress, so on their own they do not prove a craving. · Carter & Tiffany (1999), Addiction

2

How long it lasts

Builds, peaks and passes, usually within 15 to 30 minutes.

Open

How long a craving lasts

Therapists often describe a craving as an urge that builds, peaks and passes, usually within about 15 to 30 minutes.

Length and strength differ from person to person. It is not yet known exactly how to tell a craving apart from everyday stress.

3

Sleep and craving

Each makes the other worse. Poor sleep is linked to stronger cravings and relapse.

Open

Sleep and craving

Poor sleep and cravings make each other worse.

After poor sleepStudies that follow people day by day find stronger cravings after poor sleep.
Sleep and relapseSleep problems are common in early recovery. In alcohol use, insomnia is linked to a higher risk of relapse.
Night is quieterAt night the body is still, so changes are easier to see than during a busy day.

Graupensperger et al. (2022), Addictive Behaviors · Brower et al. (2001), American Journal of Psychiatry

Knowing earlier gives the doctor a chance to step in before a lapse becomes a relapse.

Call or message

1

Each patient's normal

The first week only learns. No alerts until then.

Open

Learning each patient's normal

Normal heart rate and variability differ between people, and through the day.

First week: learning only, no alertsAfter that: watching, and still learning

The first week only learns

No alerts in the first week, while it learns what is normal for the patient. After that, it keeps updating as health or fitness slowly change.

Normal for this time of day

Heart rate is lower at night and higher in the day. Each reading is compared with the patient's own range for that hour.
